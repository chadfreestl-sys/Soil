🧭 Evidence & Confidence Layer

Evidence confidence describes how strongly the direction and mechanism of a modeled response are supported. It does not mean the displayed number is a field prediction. Turfarium is still an educational scenario model, and response magnitude is not calibrated to a specific property, cultivar, product, or application program.

v19.4 · visible on outputs
TIER A · MECHANISTIC COREStandard physical or chemical direction is well established: water/air balance, nutrient loading, pH direction, salinity and basic N transformations. Exact values remain simulated.
TIER B · SUPPORTED DIRECTIONEcological or plant response has a defensible mechanistic basis, but site conditions, cultivar and biology make the size and timing of the response uncertain.
TIER C · EXPLORATORYFormulation-dependent, biologically complex or weakly calibrated pathways such as seaweed biostimulant signals, brewed-tea community shifts and generic inoculant persistence.
Read the badges this way: Tier A can still be numerically wrong for a real lawn because this simulator is not calibrated with field sensors or lab data. Tier B and C should be interpreted primarily as directional hypotheses for comparison, not treatment claims or recommendations.

Scientific transparency

This v17 interactive edition adds data-responsive illustration and selectable overlays; it does not add calibrated biological predictions. This is a conceptual, deterministic educational model with laboratory-style input and output units; those values are not validated field forecasts. EC assumes a saturated-paste extract (ECe), which cannot be substituted directly with 1:1 or 1:2 soil-water EC. Soil-test P and K depend on extraction method and region. Bulk density and SOM are assumed to represent the topsoil layer. No specific amendment application rates or fertilizer recommendations are provided. v13 adds separate 1-0-2 soluble seaweed and 0-0-14 high-potassium seaweed formulations, with hypothetical equal product-mass reference doses; 1-0-2 and 0-0-14 refer to N–P₂O₅–K₂O guaranteed analyses, not percentage seaweed. Nutrient increments and electrical conductivity effects are illustrative, not rate-calibrated. v12 adds conceptual organic and synthetic product classes with heuristic effects, not manufacturer-specific formulations or label rates. Potassium phosphite is not modeled as phosphate fertilizer. Micronutrient deficiencies, phosphorus sorption, nitrogen source transformations and surfactant hydrology are not fully resolved. Suggested turf pH ranges are broad horticultural guidelines, not measured site-specific optima; species and cultivars vary. Seaweed biostimulant response is represented as a short-lived, environmentally conditional plant-response signal with delayed, modest root effects; the response is not a prediction of MaxiCrop or any other specific formulation, and seaweed is not assumed to add viable soil microbes. Worm/compost tea is modeled separately and no synergy is assumed. Regional weather is a deterministic seasonal scenario, not live observations, a historical climate normal, or a forecast. Month is an approximate 30.4-day calendar for the 365-day model year. Carbon and trophic feedbacks are heuristic proxies without validated carbon mass balance. v11 moisture, drainage, oxygen, and competition are bounded conceptual indices, not hydrologic or microbial field measurements; precipitation is represented through seasonal moisture proxies rather than real rainfall, and oxygen is not measured soil gas concentration. The numerical outputs are uncalibrated, unitless indices generated by heuristic equations, not measured microbial populations, earthworm densities, soil electrical conductivity, fertilizer salt concentrations, or validated turf forecasts. The “high salt” treatment represents a hypothetical transient soluble-salt stress event, not a specific product or safe/unsafe application rate. Earthworm movement, habitat stress, and population decline are distinct in reality; the model represents activity and habitat suitability, not actual migration or mortality. Fertilizer response depends on source, rate, soil, moisture, and species. The visual cross-sections are illustrative and not anatomically to scale. v15 custom product nutrient loads assume a nominal soil mass and ignore detailed chemical fate. v16 adds ground beans, cornmeal and oat bran as conceptual food inputs, plus a time-stepped oxygen and microbial-activity proxy timeline. Food-source effects on actual microbial species are not identified or validated. The brewing lab uses qualitative oxygen/activity heuristics and cannot establish organism identity, microbial safety, pathogen absence or treatment efficacy.
